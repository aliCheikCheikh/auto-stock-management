== Business context

The system manages stock for an automotive parts shop: oils, filters, engine parts and similar products.
Manual stock management causes shortages, duplicate purchases and limited visibility. The aim is a simple, reliable system that can evolve.

== Users

* Two initial users: the owner and another seller.
* Tablet use.
* Manual search by name or reference; no barcode scanning.

== Aggregate overview

The model groups seven aggregates into four core aggregates and three supporting aggregates.

[cols="1,1"]
|===
| Aggregate root | Responsibility

| `Product`
| Product identity, catalog and alert threshold.

| `StorageLocation`
| Physical location and stock levels per product.

| `StockMovement`
| Immutable record of receipts, issues and transfers.

| `Sale`
| Sales transaction, line items and total amount.

| `Category`
| Product classification (supporting aggregate).

| `Shop`
| Physical shop, allowing for multiple shops (supporting aggregate).

| `User`
| System user (supporting aggregate).
|===

== Design principles

Each aggregate contains only what it needs to protect its invariants. `Product` does not contain stock levels or movements; those belong to separate aggregates.
Aggregates reference one another by typed identifiers such as `ProductId` and `LocationId`, rather than direct object references, to keep transaction boundaries explicit.

A `StockMovement` is created once and cannot be updated or deleted. Its immutability preserves the stock audit trail.
Each `StorageLocation` belongs to a `Shop`, allowing additional shops without restructuring this relationship.
